Rapidly following the struggle with conceptualisating AI and how it fits into my understanding of the world, I've discovered something fascinating - business and marketing. What sparked the following train of thoughts would have to be the Lent Week 6 Engineer in Business lecture, where the female lecturer's intro to marketing caught my attention. What she was describing resonated with me, as I was able to visualise the stories she told, and I know that's when deep learning and critical thinking actually happen. 

Compared to others' nonchalant feelings for the business lectures and how they are not 'truly' engineering, but rather just some common sense, I've had to think about why such content would appeal to or be of any value to me. I think it offered me perpsectives on aspects of the world I have not thought about, or have not thought about in such a systematic way. From day 1 of engineering, I've already realised that I will almost certainly have to face challenges that are not purely engineering. There's so much more required to make it work: communication, network, prior experience, which seemed so daunting and unreachable at times. But over the two years, I've made a start, internally coming to terms with the trade offs from just being in this field and actively adapting myself to suit the environment as much as I could. Challenges still exist, in simple terms, not only have I found myself to no longer be the absolute 'best' at academic work, but also I am most definitely lacking the soft skills, often being key factors to many opportunities in life. 

Thinking about what I'd like to achieve, or just to even give myself the chance of achieving what I'd like to achieve, I have to address these individual lackings. I think the business lectures clicked for me, as this was a huge section of guidance that I've been subconsciously seeking. What I see through these basic theories and case studies isn't just limited to what was explicitly said, I see really human nature and fundamental rules behind the way society operates. Those are things I immediately realised would be crucial to one's construction of worldview, without which, individual idealism and fantasy are meaningless in our current world. Through this perspective offered, I felt I was for the first time able to break into a world, where I could perhaps exert my power on decision making that had actual meaning. This could sound too vague, but basically: I appreciate the way the case studies probe deeper thoughts on societal values and our success (at least for a business model) depends on how well we interpret and understand these, which all boil down to human nature. I believe engineering builds on this. The lecturer said: "If a product does not meet the needs of a market, then it would not be an innovation, it would be an invention." For a purist, there could be philosophical debate on the value of such an invention, where I would argue still holds its value, but has little lingering influence. 

Before entering my degree, I would declare my ultimate pursuit to be learning about physics and maths, to enrich one's knowledge (and perhaps, do something with it). In first year, I soon realised engineering is far from pure sciences and learnt to embrace that (with periods of doubt), but I would still have argued the significance of technical knowledge in setting the bar for something's value. Now, I finally understood where engineering might stand among all this. Engineering, being my one and only major of choice, is a small window, a mechanism, a lens, through which I have come to be exposed to the world. I see things in engineering context and I do them in the role of an engineer (maybe), but is that enough? Is that my life goal fulfilled? There are missing pieces and limitations to how much I can see and can do. 

That leads me to my new definition of life goal. It has shifted from a pure knowledge based outcome to having a more wholistic understanding of the world and how it works. I am curious and would like to explore a bit more. I still LOVE engineering and I am still very determined in saying that it is the BEST degree - it's the best lens and entry point for anyone to begin understanding the world. I undoubtedly enjoy the logic tracing in the subject, where my intuition and visualisation based approach may not always be the most efficient, but is personally the most rewarding learning process. It doesn't take much for me to become 'bored' (not that I am...), and it also doesn't take much to remind me how intrinsically curious I am, about anything. At this age, I am more intrigued by the possibilities, and given the chance, I will jump onto anything. 

Final words, tying back to the purpose of this webpage: Write to think, think to write, push the boundaries, dare to be more. (sounds like some ad slogan)
